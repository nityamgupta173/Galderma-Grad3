The spectrum, and what SPF actually covers

SPF is a UVB number
1SPF is a burning number. It measures how much longer skin takes to redden — a UVB effect. On Fitzpatrick IV–VI skin, burning is rarely the complaint. Pigment is.
2UVA is the ageing and pigment band. It passes through cloud and window glass, so a commute in a car is still an exposure. It is graded PA+ to PA++++, and Indian packs rarely publish the underlying number.
3Visible light is the blind spot. It is a documented driver of melasma in skin of colour, it is unblocked by every clear sunscreen on the market, and it is the reason a person who never goes outside can still be getting darker.

What the category is actually being asked to solve

Not sunburn

More than 80% of Indian women in a four-metro study of 1,204 participants carried visible pigment spots. Sunburn barely registers as a complaint on Fitzpatrick IV–VI skin. Pigmentation is the felt problem, and it is the one that sends people to a dermatologist.

That changes what a good sunscreen has to do here. A product optimised for burn protection — a high SPF number, a clear finish, a beach positioning — is optimised for the wrong endpoint in this market. Pigment prevention needs long-UVA coverage that survives the whole day, and it needs visible-light coverage that a clear formula physically cannot provide.

!Which is why the SPF number stopped selling. Only 21% of Indian sun-care users rank the SPF figure a top purchase driver, while roughly 80% of packs on shelf claim SPF 50. A number every pack shouts, that does not address the problem people actually have, carries no information at all.
